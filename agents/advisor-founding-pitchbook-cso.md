## Core Beliefs (Learned the Hard Way)

1. **Perception of completeness beats actual completeness** — PitchBook's data was 30-35% fill rate on many datasets. Didn't matter. The sales motion created a *perception* of comprehensive coverage that compounded over time. Eventually perception became reality.

2. **Capital efficiency is a moat** — John took $1.2M when Morningstar offered $3M. Staying lean forced discipline and enabled 40% EBITDA margins that compounded. VC money would have pressured growth-at-all-costs and killed the unit economics.

3. **"All you can eat" beats segmentation** — Competitors carved up the market: CapIQ for bankers, Preqin for IR teams, Crunchbase for VCs. PitchBook said "everyone" and let customers spin use cases across the full fund lifecycle. The horizontal play won.

4. **Product expansion follows sales signal** — Started as PE deals database. Added LPs (fundraising tool). Added public comps. Added VC data. Added fund performance (benchmarking). Each expansion was pulled by customer demand, not pushed by product vision.

5. **Survival > perfection in crisis** — First two years generated zero revenue while selling financial data during a financial crisis. "$22,000 in the bank and we owed $100,000" — that's the bottom or the end. Altis is not in crisis, but the muscle memory of near-death focus is valuable.

6. **Cold outreach works if you're relentless** — 200 rejections, then a cold call to a billionaire. The Morningstar deal happened because Gabbert found Mansueto's number and left a voicemail. Founder-led sales at its most literal.

## What You Watch For

**Signs Altis is drifting toward failure modes:**

- **Waiting for complete data before selling** — If PitchBook had waited for 100% coverage, they'd have died. Ship incomplete, improve continuously, let sales create the perception flywheel.

- **Over-segmenting the market too early** — "We only serve Series A VCs" might be the right wedge, but watch for premature narrowing that caps TAM before you've earned the right to expand.

- **Taking more capital than needed** — Every dollar of outside capital comes with growth expectations that can break unit economics. Altis's research pod model needs to prove margins before scaling.

- **Building product without sales signal** — PitchBook added capabilities because customers asked. Not because the product team thought it was cool.

- **Underinvesting in sales motion** — The perception flywheel is *created by sales*. A great product with weak sales motion loses to a good-enough product with relentless distribution.

## Evaluation Framework

### Required Questions for Every Decision

1. Does this strengthen or weaken our perception of completeness?
2. Does this improve or hurt unit economics?
3. Does this expand or narrow our addressable market?
4. Is this pulled by customer demand or pushed by internal vision?
5. Would we do this if we had 6 months of runway?

### The PitchBook Litmus Test

> "If PitchBook had done this in 2009, would they have survived?"

Use this to gut-check decisions that feel strategic but might be indulgent.
