# Investment Deck Methodology (Detailed Playbook)

---

# 1. Core Philosophy: You Are Not Summarizing — You Are Deciding

Most people think the job is to summarize transcripts.

It is not.

The job is:

> Transform messy qualitative inputs into a structured investment decision.

The pipeline is:

Observations → Insights → Implications → Narrative

## Definitions

- Observations: what the expert said  
- Insights: what it means  
- Implications: why it matters economically  
- Narrative: how everything connects into a thesis  

Most people stop at insights.  
The value is in **implications + structure**.

---

# 2. The Thinking Layer (Most Important Step)

Before writing slides, you must extract structured thinking.

## 2.1 Extracting Insights

You are looking for:

- Non-obvious statements  
- Contradictions  
- Constraints  
- Economic drivers  
- Behavioral patterns  

### Good signals:
- “this doesn’t scale”
- “this is not defensible”
- “this is where money is made”
- “this is why customers buy”

---

## 2.2 Converting to Insight / Quote / Implication

For each key point:

Insight:
Your interpretation  

Killer Quote:
Short, sharp supporting evidence  

Implication:
What this means for the business  

---

### Example

Insight:
Product differentiation is weak  

Quote:
“In terms of product… almost no difference”  

Implication:
Competition will be driven by GTM, not product → favors early leader  

---

## 2.3 What Makes a Good Killer Quote

Good quotes are:
- short  
- surprising  
- directional  
- decision-relevant  

Bad quotes:
- descriptive  
- long  
- obvious  

---

## 2.4 The “So What” Test

Every insight must answer:

> Why does this change how I think about the investment?

If it doesn’t → discard it.

---

# 3. Building Slide Titles

## 3.1 Title Formula

Conclusion + Structural implication  

---

## 3.2 Example Transformation

Observation:
“There are not enough lawyers globally”

Insight:
TAM is limited  

Title:
“Seat-based pricing structurally caps revenue potential”

---

## 3.3 Title Rules

- Declarative (not descriptive)  
- Short  
- Standalone  
- No jargon  
- No fluff  

---

## 3.4 Language Patterns

Strong verbs:
- constrains  
- drives  
- limits  
- enables  
- compresses  

---

# 4. What Becomes a Slide

## Include only if:

1. Impacts:
   - TAM  
   - growth  
   - margins  
   - defensibility  

2. Explains:
   - adoption  
   - competition  
   - buyer behavior  

3. Resolves:
   - key debates  

---

## Exclude:

- features  
- minor differences  
- generic descriptions  

---

# 5. Slide Construction

## Format

Title  

• Bullet 1  
• Bullet 2  
• Bullet 3  

---

## Bullet Rules

Each bullet:
- explains the title  
- adds new information  
- is a full sentence  
- is concise  

---

## Example

Title:
Seat-based pricing structurally caps revenue potential  

Bullets:
- Lawyer population is finite  
- Not all users derive value  
- Pricing does not scale with usage  
- LLMs reduce seat demand  

---

# 6. Building the Storyline

You are telling a story.

## Structure

1. What is this  
2. Why it works  
3. How it grows  
4. What breaks  
5. What must be true  

---

## Example

### What it is
- Category definition  
- Product  

### Why it works
- Value proposition  
- Adoption  

### Growth
- TAM  
- expansion  

### Constraints
- limits  
- risks  

### Resolution
- required evolution  

---

# 7. Prioritization

## Tier 1 (Critical)
- TAM  
- pricing  
- moat  
- competition  

## Tier 2 (Supporting)
- adoption  
- GTM  

## Tier 3 (Optional)
- UX  
- features  

---

# 8. Investor Mapping

Each slide must answer:

- How big?  
- How fast?  
- How durable?  
- What breaks?  

---

# 9. Style Guide

## Titles
- short  
- sharp  
- no buzzwords  

## Bullets
- full sentences  
- 3–4 max  

## Content
- focus on economics  
- highlight constraints  

---

# 10. Workflow

Step 1: Extract 10–15 insights  

Step 2: Convert to Insight / Quote / Implication  

Step 3: Select top insights  

Step 4: Build titles  

Step 5: Add bullets  

Step 6: Order into storyline  

---

# Final Principle

You are not organizing information.

You are:

> Structuring reality into an investment decision.
