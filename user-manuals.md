# User Manuals

Last updated: 2026-04-08

## Current position

Team user manuals capture how each person actually works — communication preferences, pet peeves, feedback style, quirks. These are self-authored and updated by each team member. Used by advisor agents for leadership coaching context.

## Team manuals

**Ben Sun** — Hard time building close relationships with co-workers (self-described). Learned from Community Connect that he kept mediocre players too long because of personal relationships. Struggles with the balance of personal warmth vs. holding people to high performance. Feels spread thin and hates not diving deep into details. Driven nuts by arrogance, inefficiency, politics, passive-aggressiveness, and whining. Values: wanting to win, intelligence, hustle, empathy, independence. Best way to communicate: in person, honest and candid. Best way to convince him: clear point of view backed by data. Gives and receives feedback directly — no sugar-coating. Sends emails/Slack in the middle of the night. Earns gold stars through: working smart, intellectual contribution, managing up effectively, independence, being earnest and direct. Key misunderstanding to avoid: his lack of effort in personal relationship-building doesn't mean he doesn't care or respect you. He argues his opinion hard — it's not meant to cut people down.

**Jason Seu** — Introvert who needs alone time to decompress. Laser-focuses on things that interest him. Driven nuts by office politics, lack of accountability, and chaos (abrupt changes without reason). Early riser (7:30-8:30am ET), online after leaving office. Values actions matching words above all — if you say you'll send something by Friday, send it by Friday. Wants people to solve problems, not just state them. Comes off as reserved and quiet but genuinely cares about people's development. Best way to communicate: verbal for quick decisions, written (email/Slack) for things requiring deeper thought — he responds more thoughtfully to text. Best way to convince him: show how the task fits the bigger picture. Gives and receives feedback directly, specifically, and with examples. Coaches by building trust first — views feedback as coaching.

**Stanley Gu** — Navigating dual identity (pre-US confident/assertive vs. US self-conscious). Exploring beyond pure engineering toward entrepreneurship. Driven nuts by lack of curiosity, inefficiency, entitlement, and extended waiting. Tends toward abstract/conceptual/big-picture thinking before getting to implementation — appreciates when people ground him. Values transparency, honesty, proactiveness, and closing loops. Best way to communicate: Slack >> email, ideal flow is Slack context + TLDR then follow up in person. Strong preference for written artifacts (Google Docs/Slides/Sheets). Best way to convince him: acknowledge prior assumptions, then lay out clearly why a new direction is more logical or better supported by data. Not the best storyteller (resorts to colder, more objective communication) but is extraverted at heart (ENTJ/ENTP). Coaches by trusting people — scope and plan together, avoid micromanaging.

**Mehdi Lazrak** — More sensitive to status and external validation than he'd like to admit. Very confident when prepared, anxious when not. Independent to a fault — defaults to "I'll just handle it" even when sharing would be smarter. High standards and blunt delivery. On the autism spectrum — direct and candid communication style, debates points intensely, not personal. Driven nuts by lack of prioritization, vagueness, and performative/optics-oriented work. Works in bursts of deep focus, pauses notifications during those bursts. Very talkative, enjoys debate. ENTJ — gravitates toward strategy over minutiae, not always the most organized. Best way to communicate: Slack for real-time/quick, email for context/decisions/action items. Best way to convince him: make it concrete — goal, rationale, tradeoffs, and how it ties to priorities. Gives and receives feedback direct, specific, actionable, in real time.

**Olivia Ozino Caligaris** — Moved a lot growing up, very aware of different social norms and perspectives, non-judgmental. Tends to undersell herself and attribute success to luck. Self-conscious about her English (native Spanish, schooled in French). Ex-McKinsey, noticed she became more impatient there but prefers being warm — doesn't think you have to be cut-throat to be competent. Driven nuts by inconsideration, condescension, lack of curiosity, and herd thinking. Holds a high bar for certainty — reluctant to state something as fact unless 100% sure, which can read as low-conviction. Signals confidence levels explicitly ("I have a hypothesis" = more conviction than "directionally I believe"). Does imperfect first drafts deliberately to push from analysis to action. Values transparency and responsiveness (not instant answers, but "I saw this, I'll get back to you in X time"). Best way to communicate: Slack for fast, call for immediate, email for bulky items. Best way to convince her: explain why it's important. Gets nervous about small things but is the calmest person in the room during real crises.

**Maxine Litre** — Thrives on great teamwork ("the alchemy of it — the sum being greater than the parts"). Values clear, honest, direct communication above almost everything — sometimes too direct, something she's actively working on. Most productive in mornings, brain-dead in late afternoons, second wind in evenings. Extremely loyal — will go to the mat for her people if they've been wronged. Believes in accountability above almost anything else: own your wins, own your misses, own the work. Family lives abroad, spending time with them is a top personal priority. Driven nuts by long-winded/unstructured explanations, lack of accountability, passivity, nitpicking, and arrogance masking insecurity. ENTJ and proud of it. Moves and talks fast — appreciates being slowed down. Earns gold stars through: leading with the "so what" then detail, being accountable especially when things go wrong, improving her thinking through debate, and making things fun. Values directness, intellectual sharpness, humor and lightness, honesty, and bias for action. Key misunderstanding: her directness is about speed, not dismissiveness — call her out if it lands wrong. When she probes deep into your work, it means she's invested, not trying to catch you out. Humor (sometimes sarcastic/sharp) is how she makes work feel fun. Best way to communicate: Slack for quick (hyper-responsive), email for structured updates, live conversation for debates/decisions/thorny issues, text after hours. Best way to convince her: lay out the "why" with context/stakes/tradeoffs, then a clean structured case — TLDR first, bullets after. Coaches by hiring smart people, treating them like adults, setting clear development goals, and being in the trenches when needed. Gives and receives feedback direct, specific, unvarnished, and close to the moment.

**Ray Vinjamuri** — Has ADHD (largely attentional, currently unmedicated). Sleep is his #1 non-negotiable — processing speed drops noticeably below 7.5 hours. Needs gym time after work. Laid back, midwest roots. Driven nuts by corporate politics, churn/wasteful effort, unclear communication, nitpicking, passive aggressiveness, condescension, "half-answers" that push thinking upstream instead of resolving it, unstated expectations, defensiveness when work is challenged. Prefers direct answers and direct feedback — reduces mental load and avoids decoding errors. Quirks: thoroughness, attention to detail, spelling/grammar. Earns gold stars through: being explicit about intent and context (especially sensitive topics — prefers direct statement over implication), setting the bar for "great" upfront so he can adapt quickly. Values culture and camaraderie — goes the extra mile for teams where he has psychological safety, checks out where he doesn't. Low ego, reliable, emotionally consistent, assume best intentions. Key misunderstanding: can over-index on getting the "right" answer vs moving quickly — helpful to align early on speed vs precision. Always seeking truth, not being right ("getting to the right answer means upgrading my software"). Night owl — prefers starting later, usually last in the office. Best way to communicate: Slack (time to think), equally good in-person for efficiency. Earbuds = deep work, use Slack unless urgent. If priorities shift, explicit re-ranking helps — otherwise pushes everything in parallel. Best way to convince him: explain the broader purpose. Feedback: prefers instant but asks that you check if he's open first — sometimes needs to finish processing before context-switching.

**Sophia Zhang** — Focuses on the negative in pursuit of excellence; perceived lack of overlap between being happy and being motivated to improve. Holds everyone to very high standards — needs an environment where best intentions are assumed. Has ADHD: hyperfixates (good and bad), relies heavily on habits/deadlines/planning ahead, can interrupt from excitement. Raised frugally with heavy investment in education — drives a sense of obligation to make the most of opportunity. Driven nuts by unresponsiveness (anxious when messages left on seen — even a reaction helps), unfair treatment, people not trying their best, grammar/spelling mistakes ("they hurt me on a physical level"). Quirks: always makes sure people feel included, thoroughness, dry humor, processes emotions by talking but is a visual learner (takes notes during calls). Passions: F1, fragrance, tennis, education reform. Earns gold stars through: actions matching words, team effort and checking in, specific positive feedback. Values low ego, reliability, emotional consistency, best intentions. Key misunderstanding: high bar even for first passes — tell her when rough sketch is enough. Tends to underestimate time on tasks. Can be very blunt/unfiltered; enjoys playing devil's advocate even when she doesn't hold the stance. In-progress: being less defensive when receiving feedback. Best way to communicate: written text/visual aids, Slack for most things, prefers questions over Slack vs grabbed live (takes time to context-switch), takes non-group-relevant topics offline/1:1. Best way to convince her: explain how it connects to the larger picture. Feedback: promptly but not rushed or out-of-context; ideally time to process after if it's big.

## Leadership dynamics summary

- **Ben:** Values independence, hates being spread thin. Don't bring problems without proposed solutions. He argues hard — that's engagement, not dismissal.
- **Jason:** Values reliability, hates chaos. Follow through on commitments. Use written communication for anything substantive.
- **Stanley:** Values transparency, hates inefficiency. Ground abstract discussions with specifics. Acknowledge existing assumptions before proposing changes.
- **Mehdi:** Values decisiveness, hates vagueness. Be concrete and tie everything to priorities. His bluntness is how he processes, not a personal attack.
- **Olivia:** Values being taken seriously, hates condescension. Don't mistake calibrated certainty for low conviction. Explain the "why" before the "what."
- **Maxine:** Values accountability, hates passivity. Lead with the "so what." Debate sharpens her thinking; she respects pushback.
- **Ray:** Values clarity and camaraderie, hates half-answers and unstated expectations. Set the bar for "great" upfront. Align on speed vs precision.
- **Sophia:** Values fairness and effort, hates unresponsiveness and slacking. Acknowledge messages. Tell her when rough is enough. Connect tasks to the larger picture.
